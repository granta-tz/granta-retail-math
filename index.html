Guide – How to Use Each Calculator

Simple explanation of what each tab is for and how to use it in your retail business.

1. Markup

What it is used for: To decide the selling price of a product based on its cost, or to check how much profit (markup) you are making on a product.

How to use:

  • Enter the Cost Price of the product.
  • Enter the Retail Price you want to sell it at.
  • The calculator shows Markup in ₹ and Markup %.
  • In the right side, enter a target Markup % to find the maximum cost you can pay, or the retail price you should set.

2. Offer / Discount

What it is used for: To check the impact of giving offers like 30% off, 40% off, or 50% off. It shows if you will still make profit or go into loss after the discount.

How to use:

  • Enter the Cost Price of the product.
  • Enter the Original Retail Price.
  • Enter the Offer % (example: 30 for 30% off).
  • See the new selling price, remaining markup in ₹ and %, and clear status: Profit / Break-even / LOSS.

3. Profit & Loss

What it is used for: To see the overall profit of your store or department. It shows Gross Margin and Net Operating Profit after expenses.

How to use:

  • Enter Gross Sales (total sales before returns).
  • Enter Customer Returns & Allowances.
  • Enter Cost of Goods Sold (what you paid for the products sold).
  • Enter Operating Expenses (rent, salary, electricity, etc.).
  • The table shows Net Sales, Gross Margin, and final Net Profit with percentages.

4. Initial Markup %

What it is used for: To plan the starting markup you need on products so that even after markdowns, shortages, and discounts, you still achieve your desired gross margin.

How to use:

  • Enter the Desired Gross Margin % you want to achieve at the end.
  • Enter expected Retail Reductions % (total of markdowns + shortages + employee discounts).
  • The calculator shows the Initial Markup % you should start with.
  • Use the right side to convert Initial Markup into Maintained Markup after reductions.

5. Markdown

What it is used for: To calculate how much money you are reducing from the original price, and to plan total markdown budget for a period.

How to use:

  • Enter Original Retail and New Retail Price to see Markdown in ₹.
  • Enter Net Sales of the group to see Markdown as % of sales.
  • On the right side, enter Planned Net Sales and Planned Markdown % to know total markdown rupees you should keep aside.

6. Sell-Through & WOS

What it is used for: To measure how fast your stock is selling (Sell-Through) and how many weeks of stock you currently have (Weeks of Supply).

How to use:

  • Sell-Through %: Enter units sold in the week and beginning units on hand. Higher % means stock is moving well.
  • Weeks of Supply (WOS): Enter beginning units and units sold in the week. Lower WOS means stock will finish soon; higher WOS means you may have excess stock.

Is this enough for pricing products?

Yes, for most day-to-day retail pricing decisions these are the core calculations you need:

  • How much markup to put on a product
  • What initial markup is required to still make profit after discounts & markdowns
  • Impact of offers (30%, 40%, 50% off) on your margin
  • Whether you are making profit or loss after an offer
  • Overall store profit (P&L)

Advanced buyers also use Open-to-Buy, Cumulative Markup, and Six-Month Plans, but the calculators above cover the essential pricing and margin decisions.
